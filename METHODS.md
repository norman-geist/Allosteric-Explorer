## Structure preparation and trajectory sampling

Protein structures and molecular dynamics (MD) trajectories were analyzed using Allosteric Explorer. The reference was the first model in a PDB file. The parser retained recognized amino-acid residues and atoms with blank or A alternate-location codes. Residue identity included the chain identifier, segment identifier, residue number, and insertion code. MDAnalysis [1] read each trajectory with its corresponding topology. The application matched the selected topology Cα atoms to the reference by residue number, insertion code, and residue name, resolving chain or segment identity when necessary. Mappings had to be one-to-one. Each mapped Cα represented one residue in the communication network. Recognized monatomic ions were retained separately in the reference PDB for visualization, without adding protein residue nodes. Ion recognition did not assign structural or functional significance. Hover inspection and residue selections preserved chain and segment identifiers. Residue-level Boolean selectors supported chain, segid, resid and residue-name constraints; topology atom selections used MDAnalysis syntax.

Distances were calculated from the supplied Cartesian coordinates without a minimum-image correction. Trajectories were therefore made whole and imaged before analysis [specify procedure]. Frames were selected with a fixed stride, starting at input frame zero. When the resulting list exceeded the frame cap, the application retained frames at approximately evenly spaced positions in that list. Cluster-specific calculations used the exact selected frame indices, which could be irregular in time. Unless stated otherwise, each retained frame carried equal weight. Communication and PCA required at least 20 retained frames. Where structural alignment was required, a rigid Kabsch fit [2] superposed the selected Cα atoms on the reference PDB with equal atom weights.

## Pocket discovery and persistence

Cavities were identified with fpocket [3] using the full protein assembly or an isolated chain as specified for each analysis. The pocket search used the protein atoms from the parsed reference structure. Water and bound ligands were excluded. Retained ions were also excluded from the fpocket input. For contextual inspection, the interface reported ions within 5 Å of any pocket alpha-sphere center using direct Euclidean distances; this criterion did not establish coordination or occupancy and did not alter calculated pocket geometry, volume, or rankings. Reference ion coordinates were not carried over into trajectory-frame reconstructions. Communication and PRS remained protein Cα models without explicit ion nodes. Detected pocket lining residues were mapped back to the reference identities, and pocket score, druggability, and volume were taken from the fpocket output when available. The adjustable settings were the minimum and maximum alpha-sphere radii, minimum alpha-sphere count per pocket, clustering distance, and clustering linkage. Parameters left unset were inherited from the installed fpocket version.

For pocket tracking, fpocket was run once per sampled trajectory frame with the chain scope and detector settings of the reference scan. Each frame was reconstructed using trajectory coordinates for the reference atoms. Missing reference heavy atoms in the scan scope caused the analysis to stop. Each visible reference pocket was matched independently to the candidate with the greatest Jaccard similarity to its reference lining set if it met the specified threshold, where $`J(L_0,L_t)=|L_0\cap L_t|/|L_0\cup L_t|`$. Two reference pockets could match the same candidate. Detection occupancy was the fraction of sampled frames with a qualifying match. Mean volume was the arithmetic mean across matched frames with reported volumes. The original PDB was excluded from both summaries. Occupancy estimates depend on the detector settings, sampling, and the lining-overlap criterion.

For inspection of selected snapshots, a new fpocket scan on the sampled frame used the original chain scope and parameters and reported every detected pocket. The lining-overlap threshold applied to tracking the reference pocket.

## Residue coupling and communication networks

For displacement dynamic cross-correlation (DCC), each frame was aligned using mapped Cα atoms outside Region A. Let $`\Delta\mathbf{x}_i(t)=\mathbf{x}_i(t)-\langle\mathbf{x}_i\rangle`$ be the centered aligned displacement of residue $`i`$. The vector cross-correlation [4] was calculated as

```math
r_{ij}=\frac{\langle\Delta\mathbf{x}_i\cdot\Delta\mathbf{x}_j\rangle}
{\sqrt{\langle\|\Delta\mathbf{x}_i\|^2\rangle\langle\|\Delta\mathbf{x}_j\|^2\rangle}}.
```

For contact-change correlation, the feature for residue $`i`$ was its per-frame Cα contact count, $`n_i(t)=\sum_{j\ne i}\mathbb{1}[d_{ij}(t)<d_c]`$, where $`d_c`$ was the selected cutoff. The signed coupling $`r_{ij}`$ was the Pearson correlation of the two contact-count series. Correlations with effectively zero denominator were recorded as zero. Contact-count correlations summarize the covariation of local coordination. Individual changing contacts were analyzed separately by comparing contact occupancies between conformational groups.

For each candidate residue $`i`$, coupling to Region A was summarized by $`|A|^{-1}\sum_{j\in A}|r_{ij}|`$. The signed mean was recorded separately. Without Region B, candidate residues outside A could be filtered by network reachability, accessibility, or the time average of the minimum per-frame Cα distance to A. When B was specified, its residues served as endpoints and these candidate filters were bypassed.

Solvent-accessible surface area (SASA) was calculated using heavy-atom Shrake–Rupley sampling [5] with a 1.4 Å probe and the application's element-specific van der Waals radii. Points were distributed on the probe-expanded atomic spheres using a golden-angle sequence. Points occluded by other probe-expanded spheres were excluded. Exposed atom areas were summed per residue and averaged over frames sampled at approximately evenly spaced positions in the retained list. The shielding set contained the user-selected shielding atoms and the heavy atoms of all mapped residues. Surface residues were defined by a mean SASA at or above the specified threshold in Å².

The undirected communication graph used both persistent spatial contacts and coupling. For each pair, contact occupancy $`p_{ij}`$ was the fraction of retained frames with $`d_{ij}<d_c`$. An edge required positive occupancy and nonzero absolute coupling, with both meeting the selected thresholds. The edge cost was

```math
w_{ij}=-\ln\left(\min\left\{\max\left[p_{ij}|r_{ij}|,10^{-12}\right],1-10^{-9}\right\}\right).
```

Multi-source Dijkstra search found the minimum sum of edge costs from any Region A residue to each reachable endpoint. The app could enumerate up to five lowest-cost loopless alternatives for a selected endpoint, allowing different starts in A but no further visits to A. The approach follows the general use of correlation-weighted protein networks [6] and applies an additional occupancy factor. The additive cost is dimensionless. It reflects both edge strength and number of steps, so path comparisons used the same network settings and examined the edge values and shared bottlenecks. Routes summarize candidate connectivity within the sampled ensemble.

Pocket participation was ranked by the sum of residue mean absolute coupling to Region A over distinct eligible lining residues reachable from Region A in the communication network. The candidate region, distance, and exposure filters defined eligibility. All eligible candidates were considered independently of the displayed top-25, top-100, or all-candidate table setting. An optional minimum residue mean absolute coupling threshold (default zero) further restricted contributors to the sum and count. Each contributing residue was included once per pocket it lined; scores were not divided by lining size. The participating residue count, strongest contributing residue coupling, and full-lining mean were reported alongside the sum. Larger pockets can accumulate higher sums, so the sum alone does not establish enrichment or statistical significance. Alternative rankings used the participating count or full-lining mean. Ties were ordered by summed coupling, participating count, and pocket identifier (omitting a repeated primary metric). Plot data exports recorded the scoring mode, threshold, eligible-candidate count, component scores, reference, and contributing residues.

The alternative pocket coupling score was the mean $`|r|`$ over all pairs between the mapped pocket lining residues and the reference set. Region A was the default reference, with an option to specify a functional reference. Residues overlapping the reference set were excluded from the pocket side. Correlations and mean coupling ranks were recalculated independently for the first and second halves of the retained list as a descriptive stability check. Comparisons with a separate run matched pockets by lining-residue identity.

## Conformational analysis and observables

Principal component analysis (PCA) was performed on the retained frames using one of three feature sets. Cartesian PCA used aligned Cα coordinates for the selected residues, with an independently selectable fit core. Backbone dihedral PCA [7] represented each selected φ or ψ angle by its sine and cosine. Backbone continuity was assessed using adjacent mapped residues from the same chain and topology segment with consecutive topology residue indices and a C–N separation of at most 2.0 Å in the first retained frame. Angles undefined in any retained frame were omitted.

The application's contact-contact PCA (ccPCA) uses a paired-distance representation. Atoms from two MDAnalysis selections were retained in topology order. For group sizes $`n_1,n_2`$, $`m=\min(n_1,n_2)`$ pairs were created by taking the first $`m`$ entries of each group after striding by $`\lfloor n_g/m\rfloor`$ from its first atom and pairing by position. For frame $`t`$, feature $`k`$ was $`d_k(t)/\bar d(t)`$, where $`d_k(t)`$ was the Euclidean distance for pair $`k`$ and $`\bar d(t)`$ was the mean across the $`m`$ pairs in that frame. Features depended on the exact atom selections and topology ordering.

In each PCA mode, the resulting features were mean-centered across frames without variance scaling. Singular value decomposition gave PC scores $`U\Sigma`$. Explained variance fractions were computed relative to the sum of squares of the complete centered feature matrix. Clustering used the specified leading PC scores and Euclidean distances. Options were k-means with k-means++ initialization (best within-cluster sum of squares over six seeded fits, up to 60 iterations per fit), Ward hierarchical clustering, and DBSCAN. For DBSCAN, all scores were divided by one common scale, the root-mean-square radius of the retained projection, before applying the selected neighborhood radius and minimum sample count. DBSCAN noise remained unassigned. For each assigned cluster, the representative was the retained frame nearest its centroid in the clustering PC space.

An apparent relative free-energy landscape was computed from the raw counts in a two-dimensional histogram on the selected PC axes. Each occupied bin was expressed as $`\Delta F=-RT\ln(p/p_{\max})`$ in kJ mol⁻¹ at the specified temperature. In the raw display, empty bins were masked. For the interpolated display, empty bin centers were assigned the maximum finite bin energy before bilinear interpolation across the plot. Counts and the energies computed from occupied bins were retained for the hover readout in both modes. The figure used gnuplot’s default palette (RGB formulae 7, 5, 15). Thermodynamic interpretation requires equilibrium sampling or appropriate reweighting. The application computes unweighted occupancies.

Per-frame observables included minimum inter-group heavy-atom distance, distance between heavy-atom geometric centers, heavy-atom contact-pair counts, angles or dihedrals between group centers, Cα RMSD after alignment, site SASA, radius of gyration, individual PC scores, and external values joined by zero-based frame index. Scalar observables were summarized by cluster using the mean, sample standard deviation (SD), median and quartiles. Box-plot whiskers showed the observed minimum and maximum. Dihedrals were summarized using circular mean, circular SD, and mean resultant length. Scalar mean differences were also reported relative to the root mean square of the two cluster SDs. Where enabled, two-sided Welch tests compared cluster means for scalar observables with at least five values in each cluster and nonzero variance in at least one cluster. Holm adjustment was applied over the tested pairs in the displayed comparison family. PC scores and circular observables were summarized descriptively. The Welch results assume independent observations and were treated as exploratory because MD frames can be temporally correlated and clusters were estimated from the data.

For site SASA, solvent-accessible areas were summed over the heavy atoms of a fixed residue group in each retained frame. Shrake–Rupley sampling used a 1.4 Å probe and 120 sphere points per atom, with the surrounding protein included as shielding. Values were reported in Å². Site SASA used every retained frame.

Radius of gyration was evaluated for the selected residue group's atoms in each retained frame as $`R_g=\sqrt{\sum_i m_i |\mathbf r_i-\mathbf r_\mathrm{COM}|^2/\sum_i m_i}`$, where $`m_i`$ is the mass of atom $`i`$ and $`\mathbf r_\mathrm{COM}`$ is the mass-weighted center of the selection. The input selection was kept whole across periodic boundaries. Values were reported in Å.

Per-residue association with a selected observable was ranked using Spearman’s $`\rho`$ on matched frame indices. Residue features were the minimum Cα distance to a reference group, the number of Cα neighbors within a cutoff, the aligned Cα displacement magnitude from the reference PDB, or heavy-atom SASA. Distance rankings excluded the reference group. Residues used to define the observable could also be excluded, and this exclusion was enabled by default. SASA used protein shielding and 120 sphere points, with frame selection by evenly spaced positions in the selected list when the SASA frame cap was exceeded. For scalar observables, ranks were ordered by $`|\rho|`$ and the signed coefficient was retained. For a dihedral observable, sine and cosine coefficients were computed separately, with ranking by the larger absolute value. Undefined coefficients were left unranked.

For state contact comparisons, each assigned cluster constituted a separate frame set. Cα contacts were counted within a shared distance cutoff for the selected residue groups or communication-path edges. The reported difference was $`100(p_B-p_A)`$ percentage points. Group comparisons included pairs observed in at least one cluster. Path comparisons retained each selected path edge, including pairs absent in both clusters. These differences describe contact enrichment among clustered frames.

## Perturbation–response scanning

A Cα anisotropic elastic network [8, 9] was built from the loaded reference PDB or a selected cluster representative. Cα pairs within the chosen cutoff were connected by identical unit springs. The Hessian $`H`$ was diagonalized and inverted on modes with eigenvalues above $`\max(10^{-10},10^{-8}\lambda_{\max})`$. The scan required exactly six excluded modes. For a unit force in an isotropically distributed direction at source $`i`$, the root-mean-square displacement of receiver $`j`$ was $`q_{ij}=\|G_{ji}\|_F/\sqrt{3}`$, where $`G=H^{+}`$ is the resulting pseudoinverse and $`G_{ji}`$ is its 3 × 3 block. The isotropic average is analytical and does not require MD or directional sampling.

Multiple source residues were perturbed individually. Receiver maps used the root mean square of $`q_{ij}`$ over the selected sources for each receiver. Source rankings used the root mean square over the chosen target residues for each source. Both aggregates excluded $`i=j`$. Sources and targets could be all mapped residues, a pocket, a transferred communication path, or a custom group. Transferred paths supplied residue selections for the scan, while the elastic network included the whole mapped structure. The score is in relative model units and describes static linear mechanical susceptibility. A source–receiver pair identifies where the model was forced and where it responded. The response matrix has no time or route information.

The responder display and export could be restricted to all scored residues, residues outside the scanned sources, the monitored region, or another selected group. This display selection was applied after computing the response. Pocket response was summarized as the arithmetic mean of the displayed receiver scores over the pocket's unique lining residues with defined scores in the selected scope. This summary used either the aggregate receiver map across sources or the map for an individual source. Pocket membership was taken from the reference fpocket scan.
